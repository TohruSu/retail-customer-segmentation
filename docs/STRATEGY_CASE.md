# 今日の食えらび編集室 — strategy case

**Status: HYPOTHETICAL PROPOSAL.** This document retains the user-created meal-choice concept while separating simulated observations from managerial judgment. No retailer has implemented or endorsed it, and no demand, visitation, purchase, or sales effect has been measured.

## Value proposition and conditional audience

「食の比較編集ハブ」 means a **meal-occasion comparison hub**: compare several practical ways to complete the *same meal*, then leave with a short shopping note and a route to an existing food floor. A theme such as “What should I eat tonight?” organizes alternatives without assuming one format is best. Cooking from ingredients, prepared food, frozen/easy options, and combinations receive comparable space.

The editorial audience hypothesis is a shopper balancing a meal decision, limited time, and interest in comparing options. It is a design scenario rather than a recovered real segment. If permitted interviews and pilot observation show that comparison helps this audience complete a meal choice, prioritize themes for that occasion. If comparison adds friction, simplify the format or use an unsegmented occasion-based service. Neither synthetic cluster size nor silhouette resolves that choice.

| Evidence status | Statement | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation · SYNTHETIC | Computed attitude and visit patterns describe independently generated rows | Use them to demonstrate interpretation, not to assert consumer demand |
| Assumption | Some shoppers face a choice among several meal-completion formats | Investigate with permitted interviews and observation |
| Interpretation | A comparison at the occasion level could reduce choice friction | Develop an interface hypothesis; do not infer causal benefit |
| Recommendation | Pilot neutral comparisons and a short optional shopping note | Measure engagement and food-floor transitions against a comparison |
| Unknown | Reachable audience, accessibility, incremental purchases, and operating costs | Require new permitted operational evidence before scaling |

## Journey and space assumptions

| Step | Proposed experience | Measurement question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Discover | A visible occasion theme invites a choice | Who had a meaningful opportunity to see it? |
| 2 · Compare | Compare ingredients, prepared food, frozen/easy options, and combinations | Did the shopper engage, understand, and choose? |
| 3 · Note | Optional compact shopping note or accessible paper alternative | Was the note useful enough to take or save? |
| 4 · Transition | Category directions connect to the existing food floor | Did the shopper reach the intended area? |
| 5 · Complete | Select available products; compare alternatives if stock changes | Was a meal-related purchase observed with permitted linkage? |
| 6 · Revisit | Themes change on an agreed operational cadence | Did measured repeat visits differ from the comparison? |

A roughly **150-tsubo** space may be used as an optional exercise assumption. It is not a currently available site, approved project area, floor plan, budget, or evidence of feasible capacity. The journey diagram is non-architectural. A pilot can start with a smaller reversible display after access, staffing, merchandising, and safety constraints are confirmed.

## Operating risks

| Risk | Mitigation hypothesis | Monitor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparison overload | Limit visible alternatives; test plain labels | Abandonment and comprehension, including accessibility feedback |
| Outdated price or stock | Assign update ownership and timestamps; offer substitutions | Availability and display-error rate |
| Category / supplier bias | Balanced comparison criteria and visible commercial context | Distribution of exposure and shopper feedback |
| Staff burden and theme fatigue | Reusable templates, realistic refresh cadence, clear responsibility | Preparation hours, stale themes, and service interruptions |
| Food / allergen misinformation | Use verified information and existing retailer processes | Corrections and complaints; no medical personalization |
| Privacy or intrusive tracking | Aggregate measures first; minimize data; optional consented linkage | Consent rate, missing linkage, retention compliance |
| Stockouts, queues, or navigation friction | Record availability and congestion; adjust routes | Stockout hours, dwell time, missed transitions |
| Displaced sales or low incremental value | Examine category totals, substitution, margin, and operating cost | Cannibalization and incremental gross contribution uncertainty |

## KPI path and data requirements

**Exposure → interaction → shopping note → food-floor transition → purchase → revisit.** These are proposed definitions, not observed values. Define the observation unit, eligibility window, deduplication, and missing-linkage handling before collection. Report counts and denominators together; do not combine unmatched counters as if they form a person-level funnel.

| KPI | Numerator / denominator | Required data and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exposure coverage | Eligible visits with defined exposure opportunity / eligible store visits during staffed pilot hours | Calibrated footfall plus display availability; opportunity is not confirmed attention |
| Interaction rate | Exposed eligible visits with a defined comparison interaction / exposed eligible visits | Observation or privacy-preserving event counts; shared devices and repeat events need deduplication |
| Note take-up | Interacting eligible visits taking/saving a note / interacting eligible visits | Anonymous count or permitted session linkage; taking a note is not a purchase |
| Transition rate | Interacting eligible visits reaching the defined food-floor zone within the window / interacting visits observable for that window | Permitted zone observation/linkage; record unobservable visits separately |
| Linked purchase rate | Observable interacting eligible visits with a qualifying food purchase within the window / interacting eligible visits with complete permitted POS linkage | Consented linkage or suitable aggregate design; differential linkage can bias rates |
| Overall food sales per eligible visit | Net qualifying food sales in eligible store-hours / eligible store visits in those hours | POS net of returns and calibrated footfall; aggregate association needs a comparison |
| Revisit rate | Consented eligible participants with a later qualifying visit within the fixed follow-up / consented eligible participants with complete follow-up | Permitted pseudonymous longitudinal linkage; anonymous footfall cannot establish individual revisit |
| Availability | Hours with required comparison options available / scheduled display hours | Stock and display logs; separate full availability from substitutions |
| Operating cost | Attributable staff, materials, and refresh costs / staffed pilot hours | Actual cost records; no invented ROI |

No funnel rate alone establishes incremental impact. Present linkage coverage and attrition by arm/period. Avoid treating participants who chose to interact as interchangeable with those who did not: self-selection makes that comparison confounded.

## Pilot preregistration outline

1. **Question and primary outcome.** Before launch, specify whether the comparison hub improves food-floor engagement or purchasing relative to usual operation. Select one primary estimand, for example the difference in net qualifying food sales per eligible store visit between assigned pilot and comparison periods/stores. Specify qualifying categories, store-hours, tax/returns treatment, and a secondary transition measure. Do not choose outcomes after seeing favorable results.
2. **Comparison and assignment.** Where feasible, randomize comparable stores or store-periods, or use a randomized staggered rollout with enough concurrent comparison periods. Record assignment and implementation fidelity. If only a matched observational comparison is feasible, preregister matching and adjustment, assess pre-period trends, and retain the causal limitation. A single before/after store comparison is weak evidence.
3. **Baseline and calendar.** Collect a pre-period and a fixed pilot/follow-up window spanning comparable weekdays and meal occasions. Document seasonality, holidays, weather when relevant, and local disruptions. Estimate sample size/minimum detectable effect using permitted baseline data and the assignment unit; account for serial correlation and clustering. Do not use this fictional cohort as a power calculation.
4. **Confounders and operations.** Log store footfall, promotions, price changes, stockouts, assortment changes, staff coverage, display uptime, and other campaigns in both arms. State exclusions, exposure definitions, covariates, and missing-data rules in advance. Analyze assigned periods even when participation is low; show a separately labeled fidelity analysis if needed.
5. **Analysis.** Preregister the primary contrast, uncertainty intervals, any baseline adjustment and store/time effects, and secondary-outcome multiplicity handling. Use an estimator appropriate to the assignment and repeated observations. Report counts, denominators, all prespecified outcomes, and departures from plan. Interpret uncertainty rather than requiring a favorable p-value.
6. **Attribution and spillover.** Track concurrent marketing, stock effects, cross-shopping, and comparison contamination. Aggregate POS changes do not identify the mechanism; individual linkage has consent/coverage limits. Novelty may decay, and cannibalization may shift sales across categories. Separate feasibility/comprehension from incremental commercial benefit.
7. **Decision rules.** Set thresholds for acceptable operating cost, accessibility, comprehension, and stock reliability before testing; the owner must choose business thresholds using real constraints. Expand only if evidence and uncertainty support the objective. Otherwise revise or stop. No result is promised by this case study.

The next evidence should be permitted concept research and a reversible operational test, not a stronger claim about the simulated clusters.
